Learn to Crochet

Crochet is the craft of making fabric with a single hook: you pull loops of yarn through other loops, one stitch at a time. It needs very little gear, it travels well, and the handful of stitches below are enough to make scarves, blankets, hats and toys.

Tools you need

You can start with five inexpensive things:

  • A crochet hook — a 5 mm or 5.5 mm aluminium or bamboo hook is ideal for learning.
  • Yarn — a smooth, light-coloured worsted-weight yarn, so you can see each stitch clearly.
  • Scissors — any small, sharp pair for cutting yarn.
  • A tapestry needle — a blunt, large-eyed needle for weaving in loose ends.
  • Stitch markers — little clips that mark the first stitch of a row or round (a safety pin works too).

Yarn weights & hooks

Yarn comes in standard thicknesses called weights, numbered 0 (finest) to 7 (thickest). The thicker the yarn, the bigger the hook you need. Most yarn labels print the weight number and a recommended hook size, so check the label first.

Beginner tip: start with a medium (4, worsted) yarn and a 5 mm (H-8) to 5.5 mm (I-9) hook. It's thick enough to see your stitches, but not so bulky that it's awkward to hold.

Basic stitches

Almost every crochet project is built from these five stitches. Each card shows the symbol used in crochet charts, the abbreviation used in written patterns, and how to work it. Every stitch except the chain is worked into a stitch from the row below.

Chain

US ch · UK ch

The foundation of most projects. Make a slip knot on your hook, wrap the yarn over the hook (yarn over) and pull it through the loop on your hook. Each pull-through makes one chain.

Slip stitch

US sl st · UK ss

The flattest stitch, used to join rounds or move along a row without adding height. Insert your hook, yarn over, and pull straight through both the stitch and the loop on your hook.

Single crochet

US sc · UK dc

A short, dense stitch. Insert your hook, yarn over and pull up a loop (two loops on the hook), then yarn over and pull through both loops.

Half double crochet

US hdc · UK htr

Halfway between single and double in height. Yarn over, insert your hook and pull up a loop (three loops on the hook), then yarn over and pull through all three.

Double crochet

US dc · UK tr

A tall stitch that grows fabric quickly. Yarn over, insert your hook and pull up a loop (three loops), yarn over and pull through two, then yarn over and pull through the last two.

Reading patterns

Written patterns use short abbreviations. These are the ones you'll see most often:

ch
chain
sl st
slip stitch
sc
single crochet
hdc
half double crochet
dc
double crochet
st/sts
stitch / stitches
rep
repeat
sk
skip
yo
yarn over (wrap the yarn over your hook)
inc
increase (two stitches in one stitch)
dec
decrease (work two stitches together)
rnd
round (a row worked in a circle)

US or UK? Check first. The two systems use the same names for different stitches, which is the classic beginner trap. US sc = UK dc, US hdc = UK htr, and US dc = UK tr. If a UK pattern says “double crochet”, it means a US single crochet. Patterns usually say which terms they use near the top. This page uses US terms.

Keep learning

Once you've tried the basics, these free resources will take you further.

Video tutorials

  • Bella Coco — calm, slow-paced lessons that start from how to hold the hook.
  • TL Yarn Crafts — clear stitch tutorials plus beginner-friendly project walk-throughs.
  • Naztazia — a huge library of close-up stitch and technique videos.

Free patterns

  • AllFreeCrochet — thousands of free patterns you can filter by skill level.
  • Moogly — free patterns, many with step-by-step photos and videos.
  • Sarah Maker — beginner guides and simple free patterns with lots of photos.

Communities

  • r/crochet — a big, friendly forum for sharing projects and getting inspired.
  • r/CrochetHelp — post a photo of your work and ask what went wrong.
  • Crochet Guild of America — the national guild, with local chapters, classes and events.
